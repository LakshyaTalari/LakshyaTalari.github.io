Chapter 01 United Kingdom

London

8 days · March · 6°C and raining politely

It began with a night flight and a window seat, London arriving as a smudge of orange light beneath low cloud.

I walked it rather than rode it. Seven days of the Tube only when my legs gave in, and the city rewarded me for it — a brass band in a Bloomsbury square, a bookshop that had been there since 1902, the smell of roasted chestnuts at a market I never learned the name of.

The rain was constant and soft, the kind you stop noticing. Everyone apologises for it and no one actually minds.

“London doesn’t show you its best side. It waits until your third day, then hands you a sunset over Waterloo Bridge.”

Chapter 02 भारत

India

21 days · December · 24°C and every colour at once

Three weeks is insultingly short for India, and I knew that going in. So I chose depth over distance: the north, slowly.

Delhi woke me at five with horns and prayer calls overlapping, and I stopped fighting it after a day. Jaipur was pink in the daylight and gold at sunset. Varanasi was the one that changed something — smoke, marigolds, and a river that has been doing this for three thousand years without asking anyone's permission.

I ended in Kerala, on a houseboat going nowhere in particular, which turned out to be the whole point.

“India doesn’t ask you to keep up. It just keeps going, and eventually you’re part of the traffic too.”

Chapter 03 Éire

Ireland

10 days · June · 15°C, wind off the Atlantic

Ireland was the decompression. Two months of cities and crowds, and then a rented car, one road, and green for as far as the fog allowed.

I drove the west: Dublin to Galway, then the coast north to the Cliffs of Moher, then south into Kerry where the road narrows to a single lane and everyone waves as they pass. I spent an hour sitting on a wall doing absolutely nothing, and it remains the best hour of the entire trip.

It rained sideways on the cliffs. A stranger handed me a flask and said nothing. That is Ireland.

“Ireland is the only place I’ve been where the weather is a personality, not an inconvenience.”

Journal

Written badly · at airports, mostly

  1. 12 March

    Heathrow, 04:40

    Everything is shut and everyone is asleep sitting up. I have decided I am a morning person now. This will last nine days.

  2. 17 March

    Camden, raining again

    Bought a record I can't play until I get home. Worth it. The market smells like sugar and wet pavement.

  3. 21 March

    Delhi, first morning

    Five a.m. and the street is already louder than any place I've been. I stopped being tired around noon and stayed that way for three weeks.

  4. 24 March

    Agra, sunrise

    Ten minutes of nobody talking. Then the tour groups arrived and it became a different, noisier kind of beautiful.

  5. 31 March

    Varanasi, boat at dawn

    I don't have words for this yet. I'll come back to it. I understand why people don't try.

  6. 06 April

    Alleppey, going nowhere

    Two days at the speed of water. My phone died on purpose, I think, and I didn't charge it for a day.

  7. 26 May

    Dublin → Galway

    Green, then greener. The radio is playing something I'll be humming for months and I don't know its name.

  8. 29 May

    Cliffs of Moher, soaked

    A man gave me whiskey from a flask and walked off before I could thank him properly. Best moment of the trip. Ireland, honestly.

  9. 02 June

    Dingle, last night

    Sitting on a wall at half nine at night and it's still bright. Three countries down. Already looking at maps for the next one.

Three lands. One journey.

Ireland, London, India — three completely different answers to the same question. I came back with a heavier bag, a lighter head, and a very long list of places I still owe a visit.

— Lakshya
